What does your house actually cost?

Sunk Cost is a free Mac app that tracks the money that's gone into your house — furniture, upgrades, monthly bills — and shows you what you'd actually walk away with if you sold today. It's 100% private: your numbers live in a plain file on your own Mac, and the app never talks to the internet. No account, no sign-up, no ads.

New here? Start with Getting Started — it walks through downloading the app and adding your first few things in under five minutes.

What it tracks

🏠 Overview
The dashboard — total spent, what's Owned vs. Planned vs. Gone, and a chart of where the money went.
📦 Items
Every one-time purchase — furniture, appliances, upgrades — with cost, date, and status.
🔧 Maintenance
The recurring stuff — oil, electricity, landscaping — so you know what it costs to just keep the lights on.
💰 Sell Scenario & Compare
What you'd walk away with if you sold today, plus a projection: is staying, renting, or buying elsewhere the better move?

Why it's different

Most budgeting apps want your bank login and store everything on someone else's server. Sunk Cost does the opposite on purpose:

  • No server, ever. The app itself has zero networking code in it — not "we promise not to look," but literally incapable of sending anything anywhere. By default, that means nothing about your data leaves your Mac at all.
  • You own the file. Your data is one plain JSON file. Back it up, move it, or put it in an iCloud Drive/Dropbox folder yourself if you want it synced — the app doesn't do that for you, and doesn't need to. (If you do choose a synced folder, your data does then travel to Apple's/Dropbox's servers the same as any file you'd drag in there — that's their software doing it, not the app, but it's still worth knowing. Full details on Privacy & Your Data.)
  • Free, no account. Download it, open it, start typing. That's the whole setup.

Read more about exactly what that means in Privacy & Your Data.

Accessibility

Neither the app nor this site has gone through a formal accessibility audit or screen-reader testing pass, so it's worth being specific rather than claiming a compliance level ("WCAG AA," etc.) that hasn't actually been verified. What has been done, concretely:

  • This documentation site: every image has descriptive alt text, the whole site is keyboard-navigable with a visible focus outline and a skip-to-content link, and text color contrast was checked by hand against WCAG's 4.5:1 minimum for normal text (the tightest pairing in the palette measures 4.85:1).
  • The app itself: the four toolbar icon buttons (text size, appearance, privacy mode) have VoiceOver labels. The rest of the app hasn't had a dedicated accessibility pass yet.

If you use assistive technology and hit a rough edge in either the app or this site, that's genuinely useful to know about — please open an issue.
