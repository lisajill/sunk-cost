🔒 Privacy & Your Data

Your finances are one of the most personal things about you. Sunk Cost is built so there's genuinely nowhere for that information to leak to — not "we won't look," but "there's no wire for it to travel down."

There is no server

Most apps like this ask you to make an account, then send your data to a company's server so it can sync across your devices. Sunk Cost has no account, no server, and no networking code anywhere in it — it is not capable of sending your data anywhere, even if it wanted to. That's a real, checkable fact about the app's own code, not a policy promise: the binary you download doesn't include the code needed to make an internet connection at all.

That claim is specifically about the app itself, though — it's not a guarantee that your data never touches the internet in every configuration. See the next section.

Where your data actually lives

Everything you enter is saved to one plain file on your own Mac, in a private, sandboxed folder that only this app can touch, by default. In that default setup, nothing about your data ever reaches the internet, full stop.

You can instead point the storage location (in Settings) at a folder inside iCloud Drive or Dropbox, to get it backed up and synced across your own Macs automatically. If you do that, your data does travel over the internet — to Apple's or Dropbox's servers — the same as any other file you'd drag into that folder. That upload is done entirely by iCloud/Dropbox's own separate software running on your Mac, not by Sunk Cost; the app has no idea that folder is being synced at all, and still never makes a network connection of its own. But the practical result for your data is the same either way: it leaves your Mac if, and only if, you chose a synced folder to save it in.

Privacy mode — hiding numbers for a screenshot

Click the 👁 eye icon in the toolbar and every dollar figure in Overview, Items, Maintenance, and Sell Scenario & Compare instantly turns into dots ($•••,•••) — useful for screen-sharing, or sending a screenshot to someone without your actual numbers in it. Click it again to bring them back.

It doesn't reach Settings, though — Purchase Price and Mortgage figures there are unaffected by the toggle. The screenshots of those specific screens in this documentation had their numbers blacked out by hand afterward, not by privacy mode itself.

"Paste from Listing" — still no network access

The Compare tool can read a payment calculator box you copy off a real estate listing and fill in the numbers for you. It was tempting to instead let you just paste in a link and have the app fetch the listing itself — but that would mean writing actual network code into the app, which breaks the one clean, always-true claim above ("this app cannot talk to the internet, full stop") into "except for this one feature." So it stays paste-only: you copy the text yourself from your own browser, and the app only ever reads text you've explicitly handed it.

What actually gets sent anywhere, ever

Nothing, automatically. The only ways your data leaves your Mac are things you deliberately choose to do:

  • Exporting a backup file yourself (Settings → Export Data)
  • Pointing storage at a cloud-synced folder you already use (iCloud Drive, Dropbox)
  • Copying the Compare summary or a listing's text yourself, to paste somewhere else

The app never does any of this on its own.
