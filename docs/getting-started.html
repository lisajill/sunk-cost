Getting Started

Five minutes from download to your first number on the screen.

1. Download it

Go to the latest release on GitHub and download Sunk Cost.app.zip under "Assets." Unzip it (double-click), then drag Sunk Cost.app into your Applications folder.

Requires a Mac running macOS 14 (Sonoma) or newer.

2. Open it the first time

Because this app isn't sold through the Mac App Store or signed with a paid Apple developer account, macOS will show a warning the first time you open it — something like "Sunk Cost" can't be opened because Apple cannot check it for malicious software. This is normal for small, free, independent apps. Here's how to get past it, once:

  1. Find Sunk Cost in your Applications folder.
  2. Right-click (or Control-click) it, and choose Open from the menu.
  3. A dialog appears again — this time click Open to confirm.

You only have to do this once. Every time after, it opens normally like any other app.

Why isn't it notarized? Apple charges $99/year for a developer account to skip this warning. Sunk Cost is free and independent, so that cost gets passed on as one extra click instead of a subscription fee.

3. Load some data

The first time you open Sunk Cost, it asks how you want to start:

  • Load Sample Data — fills the app with realistic, made-up numbers so you can click around and see how everything works before typing your own.
  • Start Fresh — an empty app, ready for your real numbers right away.

Either way, you can always change your mind later — see Settings for how to reset or import fresh data.

4. Add your first thing

Click Items in the sidebar, then the + button. Fill in:

  • Name — whatever you'd call it ("Living Room Couch," "New Roof")
  • Category — type anything; the app remembers categories you've used before and suggests them next time
  • Cost — what you paid, or leave it blank if you don't know yet
  • Status — Owned, Planned, or Gone
  • Type — Value if it stays with the house when you sell (a new roof), Moveable if it goes with you (a couch)

Hit Save. That's it — it shows up in your Items list and counts toward your Overview totals immediately.

5. Tell it what the house is worth

Head to Sell Scenario and enter your current Home Value (a rough estimate is fine — Zillow/Redfin's "Zestimate" works) and, if you have a mortgage, your loan details under Settings. That's what turns your item list into a real "what would I walk away with" number.

That's the whole setup. Everything else — Maintenance costs, the Compare tool, backups — you can explore as you need them. Nothing else is required to get value out of the app.
